How ai-videos works

One text file in, one narrated video out. You write what happens and what is said in videos/<name>.yaml. The tool speaks the lines, has the project's plugin draw the picture to fit them, and makes an MP4 with captions, or a web page that plays the same. One command, make, does it, offline and with no AI. An AI only writes the text file, when you want one to.

A project keeps each video as one file, videos/<name>.yaml. Its package.json names its plugin and holds the settings its videos share. The tool knows nothing about cartoons: it runs the plugin in a process of its own, and asks it for a plan and then a picture.

Actors

You

Write a story or a video's file, run make (or story), watch, commit. The settings in package.json's "ai-videos" are yours.

An AI

A web chat given the tool's prompt, or Claude Code in this repo. It writes sources only: episodes from a story, a new character in the library, a video's file when you ask. It never writes a made file and is never a step of make.

The tool

The video command. Reads the video's file, the library's voices and the settings, runs the plugin, fits the clip to the voice, mixes the sound, encodes the frames, builds the HyperFrames project or the web page, and keeps each stage in a cache. It never looks inside a picture.

The plugin

The project's own module, run by the tool in a process of its own. It checks a video's file and plans it, then gives each frame's picture. The cartoons' draws: an SVG a frame, which the tool turns into pixels (resvg). Another may record: a screenshot a frame.

Kokoro

The voice: a local model inside the tool, in English, on the processor. It says each line once, and the sound is kept in a cache, so a line not changed is not spoken again.

HyperFrames and ffmpeg

HyperFrames, in its own headless Chrome, checks and renders the clip with its captions. ffmpeg encodes the clip, brings the sound to the video's loudness, and puts it with the picture.

Only setup goes online, once per machine. No command calls a model but the voice on this machine: story hands its prompt to your own web chat, through the clipboard.

Inside make

The voice sets the clock. The lines are spoken and measured first, then the clip is drawn to fit them. So the plugin is asked twice: once for the plan, the clip's events and texts at its own pace with nothing drawn, and once for the picture, at the lengths the tool asks for. Nothing is timed by hand.

This is the path of a plugin with a plan: RayCraft's tutorials are made so, and the cartoons were, until they were written as scripts. A cartoon's check now takes the plan's place, and its compiled scenario the fit's; the rest is the same (What happens at once, and AGENTS.md, "A script").

The plugin

The nearest package.json above a video's file names its plugin, beside the settings every video of the project shares. The tool starts it in that folder at the first stage it makes, and closes it when it is done.

# cartoons/package.json
"ai-videos": { "plugin": "./src/plugin.ts", "voice": "af_heart", "fps": 30 }

A plugin gives its scene (what a file's use: names: cartoon), its stage (1920 by 1080, so a cartoon is landscape), and a plan, a script, or both. The plan's part is a check of its source, its plan, and its picture: frame, an SVG a moment, when it draws, or camera, a PNG a moment, when it records; its guide and commands are its part of the prompt for a web chat. A script's part is its actions, its check of a script, its guide, and draw, an SVG of what a frame shows. The cartoons' plugin has a script alone. A project of your own has a whole small one.

How the voice and the picture meet

Each text of the plan is a line, and a line starts as the picture reaches its event: for a cartoon in beats, its beat. This is meet as it was in beats, three of them and five lines, with the numbers of its last such make:

The narrator begins 0.6 s in (timing.lead) and speaks for 1.418 s. After a pause of 0.4 s (timing.gap) the voice gets to Pip's line, at 2.418 s, so the walk begins on the next whole frame, at 2.433 s. The video ends 1 s (timing.tail) after the last line. When the picture at its own pace is longer than the voice between two such lines:

  1. holds (still pictures) shrink, down to a single frame, or the least the plan gives one;
  2. then moves play faster, up to twice their speed (timing.moves.fastest);
  3. and if that is still too long, the voice waits, and --fit says so: line 2 waits 2.31s for "gone": moves at their fastest, holds at their least.

When it is shorter, holds last longer and moves keep their speed. With no hold to take the time, a move slows down, to half its speed (timing.moves.slowest), and then its last frame holds. make --fit prints the result before anything is drawn:

Request for meet: 10.967s, 329 frames (the clip's own pace: 9.181s)
  waits          hold  1.80s -> 2.43s at 0.00s
  mo-walks-in    move  2.28s -> 2.33s at 2.43s
  meets          hold  5.10s -> 6.20s at 4.77s

The cache

Each stage's key, a hash of what it is made from, is kept in .cache/<name>/stages.json. A stage whose key is unchanged, its files all there, is taken from the cache ("From the cache" in the log). So a change redoes only the stages it goes into and those made from them, and a make with every stage in the cache starts no plugin. A cartoon from a story

From a story in prose to 2D cartoons whose characters talk in their own voices, several at once where the story has it so. The prompt teaches the chat to write each episode as a script (see Or as a script): who talks over whom, who walks while somebody talks, which sound plays when. The real example is the-race: story cartoons/stories/the-race.md, two episodes, cartoons/videos/the-race-1.yaml and -2, written by a fresh Claude (Sonnet) with no tools, given only the prompt, and taken and made with no edit by hand; its reply is kept as the-race.reply.md. forest1's three episodes were written in beats by a chat, before the prompt taught scripts, and rewritten as scripts by hand.

What happens at once

A script says what happens a row at a time, and what happens at once: a chorus, one who breaks in at a word, talk over a radio, a walk as long as the words beside it, two who arrive together or run off together, music under several scenes. The tool checks it (check, in a second, with nothing spoken), speaks each line, and lays every row on whole frames by its rules of what happens at once: .cache/<name>/compiled.json, in place of a plan and its fit, which make --fit prints. The picture, the sound, the captions (two who talk at once have one each) and the page are made from that file. The real example is cartoons/videos/all-at-once.yaml, all of it at once.

# a scene written as a script
  - background: meadow
    places: { pip: left, mo: off-right }
    faces: { mo: left }
    script: |
      tune loop                            # a sound of the library, under the whole scene
      mo walk to=right
      & narrator say: Here comes Mo.       # &: at once with the row above; the walk lasts as long
      [fine] mo say: Hello, Pip! What a fine day for a jump.
      pip say @ fine.word(jump): A jump?   # Pip breaks in at Mo's word
      together:                            # all three begin at once
        pip jump times=2
        mo jump times=2
        narrator say: And so they jump, both at once.

A folder anywhere on the disk, with a plugin that draws something else. This one is whole: a dot that slides along a line. It needs no install to make videos; npm install with the tool as a "file:" package is only for a type check or tests.

# package.json
{
  "name": "dots",
  "private": true,
  "type": "module",
  "ai-videos": { "plugin": "./plugin/plugin.ts", "voice": "af_heart" },
  "scripts": { "video": "npm --prefix <path to ai-videos> run -s video --" }
}
// plugin/plugin.ts
import type { Plugin } from "ai-videos/plugin";

interface Dot { steps: { name: string; to: number; say: string }[] }

export default {
  scene: "dot",
  stage: { width: 1920, height: 1080 },
  commands: {},
  guide: "A dot slides along a line. steps: is a list, each with name, to (0 left, 1 right) and say.",
  check: (raw) => raw as Dot, // a real one refuses what is wrong, with every reason
  plan: ({ steps }) => ({
    duration: steps.length, // a second a step, at its own pace
    events: steps.map(({ name }, i) => ({ name, at: i, span: "move" })),
    texts: steps.map(({ say }, i) => ({ text: say, from: i, to: i + 1 })),
  }),
  frame({ steps }, { span, progress }, { width, height }) {
    const from = span === 0 ? 0 : steps[span - 1]!.to;
    const x = 200 + 1520 * (from + (steps[span]!.to - from) * progress);
    return `<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="${width}" height="${height}" viewBox="0 0 1920 1080">
      <rect width="1920" height="1080" fill="#bfe3ff"/><circle cx="${x}" cy="440" r="90" fill="#f2a33a"/></svg>`;
  },
} satisfies Plugin<Dot>;
# videos/there-and-back.yaml
use: dot
steps:
  - { name: there, to: 1, say: "The dot goes to the right." }
  - { name: back, to: 0.5, say: "And it comes back to the middle, a little slower." }

Data sources and targets

Sources are in git and edited. Targets are made, kept out of git and never edited by hand: make writes them again.

A project (cartoons/)

Sources, in git

  • stories/<name>.md: the story
  • videos/<name>.yaml: the video, its script in names
  • library.yaml: the characters, each with its voice, the backgrounds and the sounds
  • package.json: the plugin, the narrator's voice, the fps
  • src/: the plugin (plugin.ts, guide.ts, library.ts, script.ts, places.ts, picture.ts)

Targets, made in the project

  • .cache/<name>/: compiled.json, clips/<name>-landscape.mp4, .mp4.frames, .mp4.svgs, sound.m4a, build/, render.mp4, stages.json
  • out/<name>.mp4, the page out/<name>.html

The tool

Sources, in git

  • src/: the pipeline (cli.ts runs the stages); the plugin's host (host.ts); what every plugin shares (moments.ts, frames.ts); the plugin's types (plugin.ts)
  • scenes/clip/: the scene that plays a clip with captions
  • .claude/skills/episode/: the skill
  • AGENTS.md: the full reference

Comes with npm ci

  • The voices (kokoro-js), HyperFrames, resvg, esbuild, GSAP, the captions' font

Kept on the machine

In the tool, for every project

  • .cache/models/: the voice model, about 325 MB, fetched by setup
  • .cache/tts/: each line's sound, keyed by its text, voice and speed
  • .cache/setup.json: where HyperFrames' Chrome is
  • out/voice-samples/: what voices writes

Outside it

  • Node 24 or newer, ffmpeg and ffprobe: yours

Good to know

  • A new word moves everything after it, so the clip is drawn again to fit; a line not changed is not spoken again.
  • The library is every video's: a character's new look or voice reaches each video at its next make, not before.
  • The plugin's folder is in the keys: a change to its code draws every video again at its next make, with no video's file changed.
  • Two runs draw the same frames: compare their .cache/<name>/clips/<name>-landscape.mp4.frames files to find where two runs part. Line by line, it is frame by frame.
  • HyperFrames' "sparse keyframes" and timeline_track_too_dense warnings are normal: a clip with long holds has few keyframes, and each caption is a timed element.
  • AI_VIDEOS_CACHE, an environment variable, moves the tool's .cache/ to another folder, with the voice model and the voices in it.

When it stops

An error names the file that holds the mistake and the place in it; nothing is fetched and nothing is guessed. The paths are left out here.

no background is named "meadw": the library has backgrounds: meadow, morning, … Correct the name, or draw a new one in library.yaml.

says use: cartoons, and the plugin … draws cartoon Write the plugin's scene after use:.
has no line to say: its plan gives no text Give the video at least one line.

check found errors (above) Read HyperFrames' errors above it. Warnings do not stop a make.

Two messages are not stops. line 2 waits 2.31s for "gone": the picture is too long for the words, so shorten the move or say more (a plan's message). Mended by the plugin: a plugin with a plan may mend what has one right answer; the video has the mended form, and your file stays as written.

In story, Enter pressed too soon, or an answer copied without its blocks, is said, and it waits again. An episode whose file is there already, with other text, is kept: delete the file to take the new one.
