# The race

Every morning Mo brings his old radio out into the meadow, and Pip comes to
listen to the news with him. This morning they are there early.

The radio plays its four little notes, as it always does before the news.
Then a voice says: "Good morning. Today at noon there is a race to the big
oak, at the far end of the meadow. Whoever gets there first wins a basket of
plums."

"A race!" Pip and Mo shout at the same time. Mo begins to boast of having
the longest legs in the meadow, but Pip breaks in before Mo can finish: "And
I have the fastest feet!" The radio goes on about the weather, and nobody
listens to it.

Something rustles in the long grass. Out steps somebody they have never
seen: a small round hedgehog, brown and prickly, with a pointed snout. Her
name is Bramble, and she lives in the hedge at the end of the meadow. She
says, very politely, that she would like to race too.

Pip and Mo laugh at once. A hedgehog, in a race? Then Tuk comes running in,
out of breath, calling that he heard it on the radio too and that he is
racing as well. Now everybody talks at once, the radio under them all, and
nobody hears anybody.

"Ready, steady..." says Mo. Before he can say "go", Pip and Tuk run off
together, side by side, as fast as they can, the way they are facing. Mo
stares after them. Bramble does not run. She turns round and walks off
slowly the other way.

At the big oak it is quiet. Bramble is there first, and she waits under the
tree. Mo comes next, puffing, and is very surprised to see her. Last of all
Pip and Tuk come running in from the wrong side, hot and tired: they have
gone all the way round the meadow.

"The big oak," says Bramble, "was this way all along." She has won the
plums, and she shares them with everybody, because a race is more fun when
everybody gets a plum. Pip and Tuk both say thank you at once, and Mo
promises to listen to the end of the news next year.
